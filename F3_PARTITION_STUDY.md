# A concrete new path: first-three-innings complete-set pricing

Kalshi currently lists first-three-innings MLB events with **three** binary winner markets: team A, team B, and tie. In the directly inspected Cincinnati–Toronto event, the binding rules say exactly one resolves YES once those innings are scored, and the event metadata marks the three markets mutually exclusive. The rule also addresses a postponed or delayed game; it does **not** promise that any possible cancellation or extraordinary settlement will pay the ordinary $1-per-winning-share result.[1] A one-share **YES-on-all-three** basket would receive $1 in that ordinary terminal state. A **NO-on-all-three** basket would receive $2 because exactly two of the three NO shares win. That second payoff identity is an additional path we now test, not a claim that it currently earns money.

The new `scan_mlb_f3_partitions.py` collects *real* live public event, series, and orderbook data. It accepts only a complete, active three-market `KXMLBF3` event on the same exchange index with the exact reviewed primary and secondary rule grammar, ordinary $1 binary notional, an unchanged cent grid, and a future expected expiration. Any new rule or fair-value exception fails closed. The complete event and series fee metadata are re-fetched **after** the three books; changed rules, expiry, status, fee, or market update time invalidate the quote. The entire sequential read must take at most eight seconds and the first-to-last book receipt at most four seconds; these are **client**, not venue, timestamps. A book needs one full displayed share at the best reciprocal bid for **each** leg; bids are not asks, and the derived YES ask is `1 − best NO bid` while the derived NO ask is `1 − best YES bid`.[2] Incomplete books, changed event metadata, valid-but-thin books, bad schema, and API/TLS errors are recorded in separate categories. The versioned journal saves raw pre/post public metadata and books with a canonical SHA-256 digest; `report_mlb_f3_partitions.py` refuses incomplete journals and recomputes every quoted basket before reporting it.

For each direction, the displayed gross mark is the ordinary payout minus **all three** one-share ask prices. The research-only net mark also deducts three conservative per-fill taker-fee estimates, using the **actual series/event fee type and multiplier** with non-direct cent alignment, and a further **$0.03 per-basket latency/slippage buffer**. Kalshi's published rounding rules explain why a nominal sub-cent fill fee is not always the cash debit of a non-direct account.[3] The public market API did not return an explicit `is_provisional` field in the verified live sample. An absent, `null`, or malformed value **cannot be assumed false**; raw arithmetic may be archived, but any positive candidate is suppressed until that state can be affirmatively verified. Quotes may vanish between reads, so even a surviving positive mark would be called **indicative and non-atomic**, never a locked profit or a trade instruction. The V2 exchange supports per-order IOC/FOK behavior but provides no three-market all-or-none basket: one or two legs could fill and leave directional exposure.[4]

**Replay-verified live public sweep at 2026-09-27 02:20 UTC:** 11 open first-three-innings events were discovered; 10 had complete accepted rules and books, one had insufficient valid displayed liquidity, and **zero transport/source errors**. All 10 book snapshots lacked the explicit provisional-status field, so none could be promoted to a positive signal even if the arithmetic were favorable. No side was favorable: best fee-and-buffer one-share mark was **−$0.09 all-YES** and **−$0.12 all-NO**. This is one snapshot, not a backtest, and **zero orders/fills** came from it. Earlier 02:08 records used a pre-review journal schema and are **exploratory only**, not eligible for the replay-verified scorecard; the one genuine TLS failure in that older sample was never interpreted as a clean screen.

The separate investigation of an official-release latency trade also checked a live CPI contract: its own rule closes at **8:25 a.m. Eastern**, while the BLS schedules the corresponding CPI release for **8:30 a.m. Eastern**. That is not a post-release trading window.[5] [6] A potential Kalshi–Polymarket hedge was not verified as settlement-equivalent or accessible, so it is not treated as an executable alternative. These investigations narrow attention to testable claims rather than presumed profitability.

A finite public-only run can be launched with `python3 scan_mlb_f3_partitions.py --output logs/research-mlb-f3/unique-run.jsonl --cycles 360 --interval 120 --until-utc FUTURE_UTC_WITHIN_36_HOURS`; `report_mlb_f3_partitions.py` summarizes only recorded event/book snapshots. The output directory must be private and the journal refuses overwrite. There is **no credential import, account POST, order, automated strategy switching, or account-risk allocation** in this study. The earlier capped live pilot is finished; do not restart it. Before any new supervised money experiment, independently verify multiple distinct, persistent after-fee/buffer opportunities, ordinary settlement coverage, per-leg fill and partial-fill risk, and an exchange-reconciled capital ledger. These are necessary tests, not a promise of profit or automatic permission for further orders.
